AI Overview

  • Quick history: “Artificial intelligence was created in 1956, at the Dartmouth workshop, by famous computer scientist John Mc Carthany. At this time, AI was used at this time to create and prove mathematical algorithms, and symbolic logic, and follow mathematical reasoning. At this time, AI was unable to learn from the data it collected, or write and improve its own code. It was unable to generate text, understand human speech or objects.
  • What it does: AI contains several different types of llm's and machine learning models, able to outpreform many human tasks. AI can be used to generate text, create videos, and benefits modern medicine.
  • Where you’ll see it: Modern medicine, banking, schooling, ai automation, and production, and much more

Artificial intelligence was first originally thought of in 1950, however the idea of ai was not officially developed and claimed until 1956, by john mc carthnay, at the Dartmouth workshop, by famous computer scientist. Artificial intelligence otherwise known as AI is used to replicate and advance human intelligence, therefore allowing full automation of certain tasks and jobs. AI is able to solve incredibly complex algorithms and questions, with complex reasoning. AI uses thousands of terabytes of trained data it has collected from other users, this allows the AI to grow and understand complex problems better and approach them from more efficient angles. AI isn’t singly controlled by one algorithm, specially trained AI's use something called a large language model, or llm for short. These models contain a large concentrated amount of data and text, which is able to summarize text, and understand human reasoning. Examples of this include closed source llm’s like cluade, gemini, and gpt, and open source llms such as deep seek, created in major tech hubs like silicon valley. Although AI is extremely beneficial to our modern and futuristic world, one ai prompt has a detrimental effect on our environment between 0.24 and 0.34 watt hours of electricity, 3 grams of carbon emission, and between 0.26 and 10 mls of water, which is equivalent to 10 times more energy than a single traditional google search. Fitness for Purpose

Ai was created to replicate and replace human intelligence, this was done for three major reasons. Economical, geopolitical race, for global dominance, and scientific pursuit, fueled by curiosity. However nowadays AI is commonly used by students, for completing assessments, and automating tasks. Popular models among students include gemini, chat gpt, and perplexity. Popular models among programmers include cluade, and copilot for github, while video generative ai’s like grok and higgsfield ai, are popular for recreating videos and altering visual reality. However AI is not only used for students to generate fun videos, images, and assessments, it is also used for large scale automation, and operations. Large corporations such as space x, and nasa use ai. The following information is taken from https://science.nasa.gov/super-intelligence-science/, however modified. Nasa uses the Prithvi Geospatial Foundation Model, which was Built in partnership with IBM using satellite and Earth-observation data to map floods, fires, and crop changes. Lunar Foundation Mode which is trained with IBM on millions of lunar image tiles and terrain data to map impact craters and locate water ice. Surya Heliophysics Model which is pre-trained on Solar Dynamics Observatory data to monitor solar eruptions and predict space weather. INDUS Suite A collection of large language models built for astrophysics, planetary science, and Earth science. They also use Holy Grail & Luna which is an internal agentic AI tool built on a secure cloud environment using Google Gemini technology to analyze data and assist mission control during events like the Artemis II mission. In conclusion, AI has several thousand fitnesses for purpose, a select few of these thousand being AI automation in factories, AI data collection for companies such as nasa, and student use for university and high school. However AI has several other uses.

Traditional AI

  • What it’s for:Industries, and large scale corporations
  • Examples: ASB New Zealand Bank, Auckland Hospital, mighty ape, amazon

Traditional ai, is unlike generative ai, which is commonly used in a browser, relies on predictive algorithms and structured historical datasets to classify information, detect patterns, and optimize specific operational tasks. Traditional AI’s fitness for purpose best suits its relevant industries, such as hospitals, banking, and ecommerce websites such as amazon, or mighty ape, this is used to recommend the user other products, based on previous user data, and already built algorithms. ASB new zealand bank uses artificial intelligence across internal banking operations, customer service, software development, and support programmes. ASB has recently been pivoting to traditional AI to streamline productivity, and minimise human error. healthcare facilities, much like auckland hospital utilize diagnostic AI to analyze medical imagery and support clinical decision-making. Because traditional AI relies on past data, present bias or hidden flaws will be repeated, therefore causing serious risk, such as unfair credit scoring, unfairly denying bank loans to a minority of groups based on historical biases. and incorrect diagnosing in hospitals, which could lead to death in serious cases.

Generative AI

  • Common usersGeneral users (highschool / university students)/li>

Generitive ai is the most common form of ai, used by 17% Kadoch (2026), chatbot being the most popular generative ai, with a total 56% of all 5.9 billion internet users in its lifetime as of September 2026 Conte (2026. Chatbot specifically is built on 175 billion parameters, which translates to roughly 350 to 500 gb’s of storage, which is equivalent to the average user's computer storage. The model data of an ai is currently used, and is still being used today, to generate content for the user, however to train the model data, chat gpt has used a total of 45 tb of unprocessed data, equivalent to 45 - 90 times the storage of the average computer user. By using unprocessed data to train AI, the AI becomes significantly smarter, and is able to complete tasks and algorithms more efficiently and effectively. Open AI’s gpt model requires 25,000 nvidia a100 gpus, however newer models such as gpt 4.5 require between 40,000 and 100000 nvidia h100 gpu’s. Each graphics card costs roughly between 30 and 40,000 dollars, therefore accounting for around 3 - 4 billions dollars in graphics cards required to run gpt. Chat GPT is most commonly used to generate text, images, write code and has endless opportunities. However there are also already existing models, such as cluade, Gemini deep seek, perplexity, and several other generative AI.

Environmental Factors

  • Massive carbon footprint - Training large scale AI models requires a lot of power, therefore continuous energy creates greenhouse gasses, and significantly accelerates global climate change, as data centers rely on fossil fuels.
  • Water use - Running thousands of nvidia a100 and h100 requires significant cooling, as overheating could cost hundreds of millions of dollars, therefore to minimise extreme heat, millions of litres of water are used daily, to counteract this issue.
  • Ewaste - Certain ai software will become obsolete in the coming years, due to advancing technology and physical computer software, therefore, when data centers are shut down in the future, millions of dollars worth of graphics cards will be destroyed, and, will struggle to break down, leaving tonnes of e waste, for our future generations to deal with.
  • Energy consumption - 1 AI prompt uses 10 times the amount of energy than a google search, which across billions of users can quickly drain the power grid.

AI has a massive carbon footprint, and is a major contributor to climate change. AI uses between 0.24 and 0.34 watt hours of electricity, 3 grams of carbon emission, and between 0.26 and 10 mls of water, which is equivalent to 10 times more energy than a single traditional google search. AI also contributes to ewaste, as the hardware used to run AI models is often discarded after a few years of use, contributing to the growing problem of electronic waste.

Data Centers

Data centers are buildings full of computers that keep websites, apps, and AI services running. They need lots of power, cooling, and a reliable internet connection, so where and how they’re built matters.

Robotics

  • Quick history: Unimate became the first industrial robot used on a production line at a General Motors plant in 1961.
  • What it’s for: Doing physical jobs that need accuracy or are repetitive, difficult, or unsafe for people.
  • Examples: ABB, FANUC, and KUKA make industrial robots used in factories and other workplaces.
  • Worth knowing: Robots use hardware, sensors, and control software. Not every robot uses AI.

Robotics is about building machines that can sense and move around in the real world. Robots help with jobs in factories, hospitals, farms, and places that are hard or risky for people to reach. Some use AI, but many follow set instructions instead.

Ethics in AI

Ethics in AI is about making sure these tools are fair, safe, and respectful of people’s privacy. It also means being honest about how a system works and having people take responsibility when it causes problems. AI shouldn’t make major decisions about people without proper checks.

Dangers and Misuse of AI

  • False information: AI can make up details or produce convincing images, audio, and video that are not real.
  • Scams and impersonation: People can misuse generated messages or cloned voices to trick others or pretend to be someone they trust.
  • Privacy risks: Sharing personal, private, or confidential information with an AI tool may expose it or allow it to be stored.
  • Unfair outcomes: Biased or incomplete training data can lead an AI system to treat people unfairly.

AI can also be misused to create harmful content, automate spam, or make decisions without proper human oversight. To lower the risks, check important claims with reliable sources, get permission before using someone’s image or voice, avoid entering sensitive information, and have people review high-impact decisions.

Conclusion

AI can be useful, but it isn’t magic and it doesn’t get everything right. The best way to use it is to understand what it can and can’t do, check important results, and think about how it affects people and the environment.
